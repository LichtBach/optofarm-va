## Refractive errors

### A1. Myopia (nearsightedness)
Recognize also: nearsighted; can't see far away clearly; blurry distance vision

Definition: Myopia, or nearsightedness, is a refractive error in which distant objects look blurry because light focuses in front of the retina.

### A2. Hyperopia (farsightedness)
Recognize also: farsighted; trouble seeing close up

Definition: Hyperopia, or farsightedness, is a refractive error in which light focuses behind the retina, which can make close objects appear blurry.

### A3. Astigmatism
Definition: Astigmatism occurs when the cornea has an irregular curve, causing blurry or distorted vision at any distance.

### A4. Presbyopia
Recognize also: need reading glasses; can't focus up close anymore

Definition: Presbyopia is the age-related decline in the eye's ability to focus on close objects, usually starting after age 40.

### A5. Diopter
Recognize also: what's my prescription number

Definition: A diopter is the unit that measures the optical power needed to correct a refractive error.

## Investigations and consultations

### B1. Routine eye exam
Recognize also: eye check-up; glasses prescription check

Definition: A routine eye exam determines the exact prescription for myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism, or presbyopia and recommends suitable glasses or contact lenses.

### B2. Ophthalmological screening
Recognize also: child eye check

Definition: Ophthalmological screening is a quick, age-appropriate eye health check used to catch potential vision problems early.

### B3. Slit-lamp examination (biomicroscopy)
Definition: A slit-lamp exam, or biomicroscopy, is a detailed, magnified look at the cornea, iris, and lens.

### B4. Fundus examination
Recognize also: retinal photography; OCT scan

Definition: A fundus exam uses photography and OCT scanning to assess the retina and optic nerve, and is used to monitor glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, and macular degeneration.

### B5. Ocular ultrasound (echography)
Definition: Ocular ultrasound is a painless imaging test used to see inside the eye when its internal structures aren't clear enough to view directly, such as with advanced cataracts.

### B6. Gonioscopy
Definition: Gonioscopy examines the iridocorneal angle to help determine the type of glaucoma and the appropriate treatment approach.

### B7. Tonometry
Recognize also: eye pressure test

Definition: Tonometry measures the pressure inside the eye; it's a quick, painless test that's essential for detecting glaucoma.

### B8. Anterior angle OCT and pachymetry
Recognize also: corneal thickness measurement

Definition: This test scans the structures at the front of the eye and measures corneal thickness, data that's essential for precise glaucoma diagnosis.

### B9. Corneal topography
Definition: Corneal topography maps the surface and curvature of the cornea in three dimensions, and is used in diagnosing keratoconus, fitting specialty contact lenses, and pre-surgical evaluation for refractive surgery.

### B10. Visual field test (perimetry)
Definition: A visual field test checks how well the eye sees to the sides while fixed on a central point, and is a key test for detecting glaucoma.

### B11. Orthoptic exam with synoptophore
Definition: An orthoptic exam with a synoptophore assesses binocular vision and how well the two eyes work together, and is used in cases of strabismus.

### B12. Myopia management and amblyopia treatment
Definition: These programs use personalized optical solutions to slow the progression of childhood myopia, along with specific therapies for amblyopia and strabismus.

## Eye conditions

### C1. Glaucoma
Definition: Glaucoma is an eye condition in which pressure inside the eye can damage the optic nerve over time.

### C2. Cataract
Definition: A cataract is a clouding of the eye's natural lens, which can make vision blurry.

### C3. Diabetic retinopathy
Definition: Diabetic retinopathy is damage to the retina caused by diabetes, which is why it's monitored through regular fundus exams.

### C4. Macular degeneration
Definition: Macular degeneration affects the macula, the central part of the retina responsible for sharp detail vision, and is monitored through fundus exams.

### C5. Keratoconus
Definition: Keratoconus is a progressive thinning and bulging of the cornea, which can be identified through corneal topography.

### C6. Dry eye syndrome
Recognize also: dry eyes; eyes feel irritated

Definition: Dry eye syndrome happens when the eyes aren't sufficiently lubricated, causing discomfort.

### C7. Amblyopia (lazy eye)
Recognize also: lazy eye

Definition: Amblyopia, commonly called lazy eye, is incomplete vision development in one eye during childhood.

### C8. Strabismus
Recognize also: crossed eyes; eye turns inward

Definition: Strabismus is a misalignment of the eyes, where one eye looks in a different direction than the other.

### C9. Eye infections and injuries
Recognize also: something got in my eye; hurt my eye

Definition: This category covers eye infections and injuries, which require medical evaluation.

## Eye anatomy

### D1. Cornea
Definition: The cornea is the clear, dome-shaped surface at the front of the eye.

### D2. Iris
Definition: The iris is the colored part of the eye that controls how much light enters.

### D3. Crystalline lens
Definition: The crystalline lens is the eye's natural, transparent internal lens that helps focus the image.

### D4. Retina
Definition: The retina is the layer at the back of the eye that senses light and sends the signal to the brain.

### D5. Optic nerve
Definition: The optic nerve carries visual information from the eye to the brain.

### D6. Iridocorneal (anterior chamber) angle
Definition: The iridocorneal angle is the area inside the eye where intraocular fluid drains out.

## Procedures and interventions

### E1. Cataract surgery by phacoemulsification
Definition: Phacoemulsification is the method used to break up and remove a cataract-affected lens using ultrasound, which is then replaced with an implant.

### E2. Minor surgical procedures
Definition: This is a general category of simpler surgical procedures performed by Optofarm's doctors.

### E3. Foreign body removal
Recognize also: something in my eye

Definition: This is the procedure for removing a foreign object, such as dust or a small particle, from the surface of the eye.

## Lenses and optical treatments

### F1. Round-top bifocal lenses
Definition: Round-top bifocal lenses have a rounded reading segment that's visibly separated from the rest of the lens.

### F2. Cube-style bifocal lenses
Definition: Cube-style bifocal lenses have an invisible split between the near and distance viewing areas.

### F3. Degressive lenses
Definition: Degressive lenses are optimized for office work and intermediate distances.

### F4. Custom progressive lenses
Definition: Custom progressive lenses are tailored for digital device use, with a wide field of view and no lateral distortion.

### F5. Custom single-vision lenses
Definition: Custom single-vision lenses offer maximum clarity through an optimally chosen usable diameter, in a thinner design.

### F6. Photochromic lenses
Definition: Photochromic lenses darken in sunlight and return to clear indoors.

### F7. Digital-protection (blue-light) lenses
Recognize also: blue light lenses

Definition: Digital-protection lenses reduce the blue light emitted by screens, helping reduce eye strain.

### F8. Anti-reflective coating
Definition: Anti-reflective coating reduces reflections on the surface of the lens.

### F9. Hydrophobic/oleophobic coating
Definition: Hydrophobic and oleophobic coatings protect the lens against water and grease.

### F10. Antistatic coating
Definition: Antistatic coating reduces dust buildup on the lens.

### F11. Scratch-resistant coating
Definition: Scratch-resistant coating increases the durability of the lens.

## Roles and partnerships

### G1. Ophthalmologist
Definition: An ophthalmologist is the medical doctor who diagnoses and treats eye conditions, including surgically.

### G2. Optometrist
Definition: An optometrist assesses vision and prescribes glasses or contact lenses, without performing surgery.

### G3. Optician
Definition: An optician makes and fits glasses based on a prescription from a doctor or optometrist.

### G4. B2B partnership
Definition: The B2B partnership means supplying lenses directly from the company's own factory to optometrists, medical practices, and optical stores.
